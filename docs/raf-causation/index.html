RAF CLAIMS • SECTION 02

Causation

Examine the causal connection between the motor vehicle accident, the injuries sustained and the loss claimed. The analysis distinguishes factual causation from legal causation and identifies the evidence required to establish that connection.

Causation Analysis

CASE 01
J M Grove v The Road Accident Fund

[2011] ZASC 55. Causal nexus, the but-for enquiry, legal causation and the limits of liability where the alleged negligent conduct did not cause the accident.

→
PRINCIPLE 01
Factual Causation

The factual enquiry: whether the accident and the conduct relied upon were a cause of the injury or loss.

→
PRINCIPLE 02
The But-for Enquiry

The counterfactual enquiry used to determine whether the harm would have occurred in the absence of the conduct in question.

→
PRINCIPLE 03
Legal Causation

Whether the connection between the accident and the loss is sufficiently close for legal liability to follow.

→
PRINCIPLE 04
Proof of Injury

Establishing that the injuries relied upon were actually sustained in the accident and are supported by reliable evidence.
