JUDGMENT

Gorven AJA

[1] There is one central issue in this appeal. A baby was born with brain damage caused during labour. Did the negligent failure of the staff at All Saints Hospital, Engcobo, to properly monitor the mother and foetus during delivery cause that brain damage?

[2] The mother in question is the appellant and represents her minor child, the baby. She claimed damages arising from the brain damage.

[3] It is worth briefly sketching the legal landscape governing such a claim. Wrongfulness and the existence of a legal duty form part of the enquiry into delictual liability. In the present matter the legal duty arose when the mother was admitted to the hospital in labour. The staff assumed a duty to care for mother and foetus during the birth process without negligence.

[4] The test for factual causation is whether the act or omission of the defendant has been proved to have caused or materially contributed to the harm suffered. Factual causation remains a separate enquiry from wrongfulness and negligence.

[5] The enquiry therefore requires the Court to determine whether the negligent conduct relied upon actually caused the harm complained of.

[6] The appellant accordingly bore an onus to prove the necessary causal connection between the negligent conduct and the brain damage.
